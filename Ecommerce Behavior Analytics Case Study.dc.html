S — SUMMARY IMPACT

Why do some shoppers buy — and most just look?

Growing profit doesn't require more traffic — it's already there. The cheapest lever is converting shoppers who are already close to buying: the ones who've added something to cart. Across 42.4M October + 67.4M November 2019 clickstream events from a real multi-category online store — the full REES46 Marketing Platform dataset on Kaggle, not a sample — I diagnosed why visitors leave without buying, and where product, CX, and marketing teams can intervene.

The data says this is a discovery problem, not a checkout problem: at the event level, a cart converts to a purchase 82.67% of the time — yet 49.1% of October's cart-containing sessions still end with no purchase at all, since one session can cart repeatedly before converting once, or never. Either way, the leak is almost entirely upstream, at view → cart.

Method: chunked pandas EDA on files too large for memory, session-level feature engineering across 9.2M October sessions, a LightGBM purchase-prediction model with SHAP, K-Means/decision-tree customer segmentation, and out-of-time validation on November.

P — PROBLEM SPACE

Conversion, not traffic, is the growth lever

View volume is already enormous — 96% of all events are views. At this stage of the funnel, the business doesn't need more traffic; it needs more of the traffic it already has to convert. That reframes this from a marketing-acquisition problem into a full-funnel behavioural diagnosis.

Scope spans two full months — October and November — specifically so seasonal noise (Black Friday) can be separated from durable behaviour. Every finding below is tied to a named owner and a concrete action, not left as a standalone statistic.

Product Manager
Owns the funnel and checkout experience — needs to know where to invest engineering effort.
Customer Experience
Owns on-site friction — needs to know which browsing behaviours signal a stuck shopper.
Marketing
Owns acquisition, segments and promotions — needs to know who to retarget and why.

E — EXECUTION (METHODOLOGY)

Built to survive files that don't fit in memory

Engineering constraint
42.4M/67.4M-row files processed in 2M-row chunks with incremental aggregation. Session-level grouping (9.2M sessions) took ~27 min/month — user_session has near-UUID cardinality, unlike low-cardinality hour/brand/category aggregates.
Model choice
LightGBM binary classifier, is_unbalance=True for the 6.8% positive rate, early stopping on validation AUC. Chosen over logistic regression: features interact non-linearly and LightGBM handles day-of-week natively.
Leakage discipline
Excluded user_session/user_id, the literal purchase count, and n_events (which encodes purchase count indirectly).
Evaluation trap avoided
predict_proba ≥ 0.5 classifies everything as "no purchase" — 93%-accurate, useless. Picked an F1-optimal threshold from the precision-recall curve instead; report ROC-AUC/PR-AUC as the primary metrics.
Segmentation
K-Means (k=6, chosen by silhouette among k=3–7) plus a shallow decision tree as a human-readable cross-check — both trained on behavioural features only, purchase outcome held out entirely.
Validation
Scored the Oct-trained model on unseen Nov sessions with the exact same fixed threshold — a direct test of whether Oct alone was enough to build on, without retraining.
ABLATION — WHAT'S ACTUALLY ACTIONABLE
ROC-AUC drops from 0.92 to 0.70 — most raw predictive power is just session intensity, not behaviour type.

Recommendations

PRODUCT / CX
Reduce brand-comparison friction — clearer comparison tools, stronger recommendation ranking. Comparison-shoppers convert worse than focused browsers.
MARKETING
Retarget the "long single-visit browser" segment — 946k users, ~57 min average session, only 13.2% convert. An already-warm audience that just doesn't return.
PRODUCT
Invest in discovery/recommendation surfaces, not checkout UX. Cart→purchase is already 82.67% — checkout has a low ceiling. View→cart has the highest possible upside.
Limitations
category_code/brand are optional catalog fields with real missingness (31.8%/14.4%); product_id isn't a fully reliable brand key; no demographic or purchase-history features were available, only within-session behaviour.
Next steps
A/B test the brand-comparison-friction hypothesis; build the live monitoring dashboard as a separate deliverable; extend the model with a Nov-trained or combined Oct+Nov version if drift continues to widen.
